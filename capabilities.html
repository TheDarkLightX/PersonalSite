Scaling · Development direction

Scaling Dana Edwards' research and engineering outputs.

I see scaling my productivity as essential to remaining economically useful in a future with AGI. Today I guide this workflow as a human. My next direction is to transfer the same research process to a coordinated swarm of agents through prompting, tools, and eventually training.

I expect stronger models, more compute, and successive iterations to make more of this process transferable. The ambition is to scale my ability to turn questions into checked artifacts, while measuring whether the agents preserve the quality of the work.

  1. Transfer the method

    Make my approach to questions, abstractions, refutation, and evidence consistent enough for agents to execute. Preserve human direction and explicit limits on their authority.

  2. Expand coordinated work

    Let agents explore and implement in parallel, with Research Kernel preserving the research record. Keep deterministic checks and independent evaluation central as the volume of proposals grows.

  3. Measure the gain

    Evaluate prompted and trained workflows against my human-guided baseline. Scale the approaches that produce stronger checked outputs within a comparable budget.

What successful scaling would mean

More independently accepted, reproducible outputs per hour of my review time, at a measured compute cost. Track defect rates, unsupported claims, duplicated work, and useful negative results as well as throughput. An increase in generated text or agreement among agents is insufficient evidence of improvement.

Current status

The human-guided workflow and supporting tools exist. Transferring the full process to an agent swarm, or training it into a model, is a development and research goal. The degree of reliable automation and its timeline still need to be established experimentally.

Evidence standard

Completed is not the same as verified.

The ledger records what can actually be established. A Git commit can timestamp an artifact; it cannot prove that no AI was used. A benchmark can reproduce a result; it cannot prove that an employer's interview process will recognize it.

Where possible, the evidence chain is: task → submission → rubric → verifier → receipt → live defense.

Completed

The required artifact exists.

Replayed

A clean run reproduced the committed result.

Portfolio-ready

The benchmark met its score threshold with no unresolved critical failure.

Externally verified

An identified third party independently evaluated or proctored the work.

Career training

One active goal. Train backward from the market.

The capability ledger is also a standing training system. There is one active benchmark at a time. If it is unfinished when another week arrives, it carries forward. Only after its definition of done is met does the system research the market and select the next highest-value capability gap.

There is no day-by-day schedule. The objective is sustainable skill accumulation and maximum employability signal per unit of attention and AI/Codex usage.

  1. 1. Observe

    When a new project is needed, inspect current remote roles and extract recurring work primitives.

  2. 2. Build

    Use AI as leverage to create one bounded artifact that exercises several valuable primitives.

  3. 3. Verify

    Human-review the important judgments, score, replay, disclose AI use, attack the evaluator, and defend the result live.

  4. 4. Graduate

    Publish only strong evidence; otherwise keep improving the same active goal.
