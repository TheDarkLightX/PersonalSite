# Portfolio essay editor

This is an original site-specific editorial protocol. It draws on the general idea of Allen Yang's evolving voice guide, the first-read approach to plain technical English, and the stylistic audits of open-source humanizer skills. It does not copy their rule sets.

## The author's voice

The author is an engineer and philosopher. Start with a concrete question or observation. State what a result establishes, then its assumptions and boundaries. Use first person when the author has supplied a personal claim, interpretation, or question. Never invent a project experience, experiment, opinion, or outcome. Explain mathematics as a process before introducing notation.

## The first-read test

1. Give the reader the mechanism before its grand implication. Show a runnable or inspectable example when possible.
2. Define unfamiliar terms once, near their first use. Use one label consistently. Read equations in ordinary language.
3. Prefer concrete verbs, short clauses, and a mix of sentence lengths. Cut staged openings, inspirational closers, routine dramatic contrasts, filler, and empty emphasis.
4. Keep a contrast when it corrects a genuine misunderstanding. The difference between finite program descriptions and potentially nonterminating runs, and between mathematical theorems and physical hypotheses, must remain explicit.
5. A diagram must encode a claim that can be stated in one sentence. Its caption must tell the reader what to look for and what the picture does not establish.
6. Distinguish proved statements, standard definitions, assumptions, philosophical readings, conjectures, and results of experiments. Do not say a simulation proves nontermination unless a finite invariant or repeated complete state warrants it.
7. Verify names, dates, links, citations, code, mathematical equivalences, diagram arrows, metadata, and mobile accessibility. Never modify technical expressions to satisfy a stylistic rule.
8. Do not measure success by whether an AI detector is fooled. Measure it by a technically competent reader's ability to reproduce the argument and locate the caveat.

## Passes

- **Argument pass:** Write one-sentence thesis; test every paragraph for new information and logical dependence; remove unsupported leaps.
- **Technical pass:** Check quantifiers, assumptions, scopes, formal statements, counterexamples, proof status, and source fidelity.
- **Plain-language pass:** Read every section aloud. Replace default chatbot cadence with varied, economical prose while preserving mathematical precision.
- **Visual pass:** Each diagram has an accessible title/description, a clear direction of reading, one falsifiable takeaway, and a truthful caption. Test long labels and mobile overflow.
- **Publication pass:** Check local navigation, canonical URL, sitemap, metadata, progressive enhancement, keyboard access, and no broken references.
